Frequently Asked Questions

RIGHT AFTER AN ARREST

First, find out where they are being held and what they are charged with. After an arrest, most people are taken before a magistrate who sets bond. Once bond is set, it can be posted to get them released.

The fastest way to understand your options is to call us. We can help you locate them, explain the bond, and start protecting their case immediately.

An active warrant means you can be arrested at any time, including at a traffic stop, at work, or at home.

In many cases, a walkthrough bond may be arranged so you can surrender on your terms and be released more quickly rather than being taken by surprise. Call us before the warrant finds you.

Bail is money or a bond that secures release from jail while a case is pending, based on the promise that the person will appear in court.

The amount depends on the charge, the person's history, and the judge. Bond can generally be posted by paying the full amount directly to the county or by using a bail bondsman for a non-refundable fee.

We can also help pursue a lower bond where appropriate.

BOND AND CONDITIONS

INVESTIGATIONS AND POLICE CONTACT

You have the right to remain silent and the right to speak with a lawyer. Anything said in custody can be used in the case.

If a detective or investigator contacts you, you should understand what the contact is about before agreeing to an interview. The safest step is to speak with a lawyer before making decisions about talking to police or investigators.

IMMIGRATION

For most people appearing on a criminal case, court is about that case, but immigration status can be a real concern for non-citizens, and it's a question worth taking seriously rather than guessing about. If you or a loved one has immigration concerns, tell us early so we can factor it into how the case is handled.

Some criminal charges can carry serious immigration consequences, including for green card holders, and certain offenses matter far more than others. This is one of the most important reasons to have a lawyer who will consider your immigration situation, not just the criminal case. Tell us your status early, and we'll keep it front of mind, coordinating with an immigration attorney where needed.

YOUR RECORD AND CASE OUTCOMES

Yes. A dismissal ends the case, but the arrest and charge can still appear on your record until you take a separate legal step to clear or seal it.

Depending on the circumstances, that may involve an expunction or an order of nondisclosure.

An expunction can erase qualifying criminal records. An order of nondisclosure generally seals qualifying records from public view, although certain government agencies may still have access.

Not every case qualifies for either option. We can review the case and explain which remedy, if any, may be available.

DRIVER'S LICENSE

A driver's license suspension connected to a DWI arrest may involve the Administrative License Revocation, or ALR, process, which is separate from the criminal case.

There is a short deadline to request a hearing. In some situations, an occupational driver's license may also be available so you can continue driving for essential purposes.

Time matters, so it is important to address the suspension quickly.
